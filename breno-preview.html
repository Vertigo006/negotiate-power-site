BRENO

The decision layer

Resolve the decision, not just the inputs.

A consequential decision may already have a financial model, market data, internal analysis and expert advice behind it.

Having the inputs and resolving the decision are different problems.

Breno focuses analysis on which path is supportable now, what happens if a critical assumption fails, and what should cause the call to change.

Existing expertise stays

You don't need to replace the experts.

Your broker may know the market. Your operator may know the mission. Your attorney may know the contract. Your technical adviser may know the asset. Your finance team may know the economics.

Breno asks a different question: What does all of that mean for this decision?

Negotiate Power brings decision-relevant inputs together without silently assuming authority that belongs to the customer or a qualified professional.

The cost of being wrong

Expected economics are only part of the decision.

An attractive forecast can depend on an assumption that has not yet been established. Breno tests whether commitment, lock-in, recovery time and downstream exposure change the preferred path when that assumption is stressed.

The question is not only which option looks best. It is what happens if the reason it looks best turns out to be wrong.

Scope

Aviation is Breno's proving ground.

Negotiate Power's deepest demonstrated work remains aviation. The underlying decision problem also appears elsewhere: organizations must reconcile alternatives, evidence, constraints and consequences before committing.

Selected non-aviation decisions may be evaluated individually where the necessary domain evidence, expertise and controls can be established.
