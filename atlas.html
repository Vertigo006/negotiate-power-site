How Atlas works

Define the decision. Examine the evidence. Challenge the answer.

Atlas is Negotiate Power's aviation decision-intelligence product. The Atlas Engine is the proprietary analytical capability behind it. Customers receive the recommendation through a Decision Room, backed by a durable Decision Asset.

Four stages

The machinery stays underneath the decision.

You do not need to learn Atlas's internal ontology to understand or challenge its recommendation.

01

Define the decision

Identify who is deciding, what must be decided, credible alternatives, deadline, constraints, and the boundary within which the answer must remain valid.

02

Examine the evidence

Acquire decision-relevant evidence, preserve source lineage, separate fact from inference, and keep material gaps and contradictions visible.

03

Challenge the answer

Test competing interpretations, alternatives, uncertainty, economic exposure, and the conditions that would reverse or invalidate the recommendation.

04

Release through accountable human review

Atlas can recommend, condition, require verification, or abstain. Consequential release and customer authority remain with accountable humans.

Inspectable reasoning

What remains visible when the answer matters.

Provenance

Evidence stays connected to material claims

Relevant evidence is bounded to the decision and source lineage remains available for review.

Contradictions & unknowns

Disagreement is not averaged away

Conflicting evidence is resolved, bounded, or carried forward as uncertainty. Missing verification is not treated as affirmative evidence.

Confidence & falsification

Confidence stays qualitative and reversible

Atlas avoids manufactured numeric precision and preserves assumptions, counterarguments, reversal triggers, and required verification.

Controlled institutional qualification

Tested for a defined scope—with explicit limits.

Atlas completed a controlled institutional qualification program designed to test whether its evidence, reasoning, uncertainty, competence boundaries, and human-release controls remain consistent across bounded aviation decision cases.

The formal program, AIOQ v1.0, resulted in INSTITUTIONALLY QUALIFIED — BOUNDED for the tested methodology and decision classes.

It does not prove that every recommendation is correct, future outcomes, universal model accuracy or portability, regulatory approval, autonomous authority, customer ROI, product-market fit, production eligibility by itself, or predictive superiority.

Decision Delta

Measure change only when the evidence supports it.

Where sufficient prospective evidence exists, Atlas can later compare the customer's pre-Atlas position, Atlas recommendation, actual customer decision, and observed outcome.

Decision Delta can record changes in decision position, uncertainty, evidence, risk treatment, timing, economic exposure, optionality, and outcome. Attribution remains bounded; null, negative, ambiguous, and unsupported results are preserved rather than converted into performance claims.

No aggregate Decision Delta performance statistic or ROI claim is currently made.

Boundaries

Decision support is not autonomous authority.

Atlas is not a generic AI assistant, chatbot, aviation news service, dashboard, aircraft marketplace, appraisal service, public safety-rating system, regulator, accredited certification body, or autonomous decision-maker.

It does not replace qualified legal, tax, financial, insurance, maintenance, engineering, regulatory, operational, investment, or other professional authority.

Specific nonpublic-data requirements and permitted use are addressed during qualification and scope. Do not send sensitive records in the initial email. See the Privacy notice for current public data-handling terms.
