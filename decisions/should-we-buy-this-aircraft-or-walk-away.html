BRENO Decision Guide

Should we buy this aircraft or walk away?

Once a specific aircraft is under consideration, sunk effort and transaction momentum can distort judgment. The decision should turn on whether the aircraft still supports the mission and price after the actual records, condition, maintenance position, title, programs, and findings are known.

Direct answer

Proceed only when the remaining uncertainty is understood, priced, and acceptable. A good aircraft at the wrong risk-adjusted terms is still the wrong transaction.

AIRCRAFT

What did diligence actually reveal?

Reconcile maintenance records, damage and repair history, upcoming inspections, engine and APU status, programs, avionics, configuration, title, liens, and discrepancies found during the pre-purchase inspection.

REMEDY

Can the issue be corrected or priced?

Separate defects that can be cured before closing from uncertainty that remains with the buyer. Test seller remediation, credits, escrow, revised price, warranties, and closing conditions.

ALTERNATIVES

What are you giving up by staying in the deal?

Compare the current candidate with the next-best aircraft, continued external access, delayed acquisition, and the cost of restarting the search.

What changes the direction?

A red flag is not automatically a walk-away. Unresolved material uncertainty can be.

Do not let deadlines, deposits, inspection spend, broker enthusiasm, or fear of losing inventory turn unresolved evidence into acceptance. The decision should explicitly state which findings are tolerable, which require a remedy, and which invalidate the transaction.

Beyond the Brief: if the aircraft only works after multiple concessions, ask whether the decision has quietly shifted from “Is this the right aircraft?” to “How much inconvenience are we willing to rationalize because we are already invested in the deal?”

Decision discipline

Use specialists for specialist conclusions. Use decision intelligence to reconcile what their conclusions mean together.

Technical, legal, tax, financing, maintenance, operational, and regulatory questions should be addressed by qualified professionals. BRENO does not replace those experts. It structures the decision around their evidence, competing alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, and the consequences of being wrong.

Beyond the Brief

Resolve the decision you brought. Then test whether it is the right decision to be making.

BRENO creates an inspectable basis for the requested decision, then separately challenges framing, alternatives, dependencies, and assumptions that may materially change the direction.
