Should we delay an aircraft purchase?

Waiting is a decision too. The right comparison is not “buy now versus do nothing,” but “commit now versus preserve flexibility while the evidence changes.”

Discuss Your DecisionExplore BRENO

Direct answer

Delay is supportable when waiting is likely to produce decision-changing information or materially improve the opportunity set at a cost lower than committing now.

INFORMATION

What will waiting teach you?

Identify the specific evidence expected to improve: mission demand, financing, availability, maintenance status, tax position, organizational needs, or market pricing.

COST OF WAITING

What exposure continues while you wait?

Measure charter spend, lost missions, executive time, schedule friction, operational limitations, price movement, and the risk that attractive inventory disappears.

REVERSIBILITY

Which path preserves option value?

Compare the lock-in and recovery burden of buying now with the flexibility and ongoing cost of staying uncommitted.

What changes the direction?

“Wait for a better market” is not a decision rule.

Define the threshold that makes waiting rational: a specific utilization level, financing condition, aircraft availability, mission requirement, maintenance event, or evidence gap. Without a trigger, delay can become indecision disguised as prudence.

Beyond the Brief: test whether the organization is waiting for information that can actually change the answer, or simply waiting to feel more comfortable with the same evidence.

Decision intelligence

Give the decision you brought an independent challenge.

BRENO structures the evidence, alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, downside, and change conditions around consequential decisions. Beyond the Brief then asks what the original framing may have missed.
