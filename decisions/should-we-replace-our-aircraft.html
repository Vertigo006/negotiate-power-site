BRENO Decision Guide

Should we replace our aircraft?

Replacement becomes supportable when the current aircraft no longer fits the mission, the cost or reliability burden is materially worsening, or the next major investment would be better directed toward a different platform.

Direct answer

Do not replace an aircraft because it is old. Replace it when the evidence says the current platform is becoming the weaker way to satisfy the mission.

MISSION

Has the job changed?

Test range, passenger load, airport access, baggage, international capability, dispatch requirements, schedule pattern, and cabin needs against the missions now being flown.

ECONOMICS

What is continued ownership actually costing?

Include maintenance events, reliability, downtime, upgrades, crew and training burden, parts availability, insurance, and the cost of missions the aircraft can no longer perform efficiently.

TRANSITION

What does replacement create?

Model acquisition cost, sale timing, overlap or transportation gaps, transition training, maintenance position, and whether the replacement meaningfully improves the mission rather than merely changing the tail number.

What changes the direction?

A major maintenance event can be a trigger, but not the answer.

A large inspection, avionics upgrade, refurbishment, or reliability problem should force a fresh comparison between retaining and replacing. The decision should still turn on mission fit, remaining useful life, market value, expected ownership horizon, and the recovery path if the replacement thesis is wrong.

Beyond the Brief: the real decision may be retain and upgrade, sell and use external access temporarily, or change the access model entirely rather than replace one owned aircraft with another.

Decision discipline

Use specialists for specialist conclusions. Use decision intelligence to reconcile what their conclusions mean together.

Technical, legal, tax, financing, maintenance, operational, and regulatory questions should be addressed by qualified professionals. BRENO does not replace those experts. It structures the decision around their evidence, competing alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, and the consequences of being wrong.

Beyond the Brief

Resolve the decision you brought. Then test whether it is the right decision to be making.

BRENO creates an inspectable basis for the requested decision, then separately challenges framing, alternatives, dependencies, and assumptions that may materially change the direction.
