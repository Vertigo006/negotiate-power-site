BRENO Decision Guide

Do we need another aircraft?

A busy schedule can look like a fleet-capacity problem even when the real constraint is utilization, maintenance availability, crew coverage, dispatch reliability, mission mix, or access strategy.

Discuss Your DecisionExplore BRENO

Direct answer

Add aircraft only after the current system has been tested for hidden capacity.

DEMAND

Is the demand persistent?

Measure repeatable mission demand, peak overlap, denied trips, displacement, recovery options, and whether the constraint appears across seasons rather than isolated periods.

UTILIZATION

Is existing capacity actually constrained?

Separate aircraft availability from scheduling inefficiency, maintenance downtime, crew limitations, positioning, and low-value use that consumes scarce capacity.

ALTERNATIVES

Can external access absorb the peaks?

Compare supplemental charter, fractional access, interchange, or mission segmentation against the fixed burden of another aircraft.

Beyond the Brief

The decision may not be “another aircraft or not.”

The better question can be whether the organization needs more owned capacity, more flexible peak capacity, a different aircraft mix, better scheduling, or a change in which missions deserve dedicated lift.

A second aircraft can solve a real constraint. It can also institutionalize a process problem at aircraft-scale economics.
