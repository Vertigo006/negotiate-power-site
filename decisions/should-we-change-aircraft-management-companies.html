BRENO Decision Guide

Should we change aircraft management companies?

A poor management experience does not automatically mean the provider is the root cause. Before switching, separate service failure, contract design, aircraft economics, owner expectations, and operating-model constraints.

Discuss Your DecisionExplore BRENO

Direct answer

Change providers when the evidence shows the current relationship is structurally underperforming and the switching case survives transition cost, operational disruption, and execution risk.

PERFORMANCE

Define the failure precisely

Measure dispatch reliability, service, staffing, maintenance coordination, reporting, charter performance, cost control, communication, and contract compliance.

ROOT CAUSE

Separate provider failure from model failure

Some problems originate in the aircraft, mission profile, budget, charter assumptions, owner requirements, or contract itself rather than management execution.

TRANSITION

Price the switching burden

Account for crew, certificates, vendors, maintenance programs, records, insurance, charter continuity, downtime, and the time required to stabilize the new relationship.

Beyond the Brief

The best answer may be renegotiation, not replacement.

If the provider is capable but incentives, scope, reporting, staffing, or expectations are misaligned, a restructured relationship can outperform a disruptive switch.
