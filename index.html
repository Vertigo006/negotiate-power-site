What BRENO is

A proprietary decision-intelligence system, applied to one consequential decision at a time.

Negotiate Power operates BRENO through scoped analytical engagements. You bring the live decision; BRENO structures the decision boundary, reconciles decision-relevant evidence and alternatives, tests critical assumptions and downside exposure, and preserves the conditions that should change the call.

We are not here to make your decision for you. We are here to make it harder to make a bad one.

The decision owner retains authority. BRENO creates an inspectable basis for the call.

Why BRENO exists

The problem was never a shortage of experts.

BRENO came from seeing the same gap repeatedly: consequential decisions could have excellent advisers, credible data, and serious analysis behind them, yet no single place showed how all of that evidence supported the final call.

The gap was between expertise and decision. Negotiate Power built BRENO to make that gap visible before commitment.

Proving ground

Aviation is where BRENO proves the model.

Negotiate Power's deepest demonstrated work remains aviation. BRENO can be applied to selected non-aviation decisions when the necessary domain evidence, expertise, and controls can be established for the specific decision.

Start with the decision, not a claim that BRENO already knows your industry.

When one decision becomes many

Some aviation decisions repeat. The engagement can start with one.

Flight departments, operators, advisers, ownership teams and other aviation organizations can face recurring consequential choices across aircraft, providers, fleet strategy, transactions and operating alternatives.

BRENO engagements preserve an inspectable basis for the decision being evaluated, including the material evidence, assumptions, contradictions, alternatives and conditions that could change the direction. When consequential decision requirements recur, Negotiate Power can evaluate whether a broader organizational engagement is appropriate.

Start with one decision. If the need repeats, evaluate whether a broader engagement is appropriate.
